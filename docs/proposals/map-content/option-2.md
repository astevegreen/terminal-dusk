# Option 2: card places first, uneven regions

Part of the [map content proposal](README.md). Decides nothing. Every number is a placeholder that makes the
trade-offs concrete; the settled points, the adjacency conventions and the home-box options are in the README.

The countries are picked so that as many cards as possible find their place on the board. That makes the
three Eurasian regions larger (eight countries) and Sub-Saharan Africa and the Americas smaller, and gives
three battlegrounds to every region but Sub-Saharan Africa.

## Trade-offs

**Cards whose place is on the board.** Of the 53 cards in the README's card table, 42 have all their places on
this board, 6 some and 5 none.

- **Some:** NATO Enlargement 1999 (Poland only), Desert Storm Coalition (Iraq, not Kuwait), Big Bang
  Enlargement 2004 (Estonia only), Sovereign Wealth Funds (Saudi Arabia only), Nordic Accession (Finland, not
  Sweden), Arab Spring (Libya only).
- **None:** Maastricht Treaty (Netherlands), Pristina Airport Dash and Kosovo Declares Independence (Kosovo),
  Asian Financial Crisis (Thailand), Al Jazeera Launches (Qatar).

**Balance of starting reach.** "Next to it" is where a side may place from the start without any influence;
"one step further" is the battlegrounds next to those countries.

| Home box | Home-box option | Countries next to it | Battlegrounds next to it                                       | Battlegrounds one step further |
| -------- | --------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| LIO      | H1 or H2        | 3                    | 1 (Mexico)                                                     | 1 (Venezuela)                  |
| SAE      | H1              | 16                   | 7 (Poland, Ukraine, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Japan, Taiwan, India) | 3 (Germany, Serbia, Iran)      |
| SAE      | H2              | 3                    | 1 (Kazakhstan)                                                 | 2 (Poland, Ukraine)            |

The extra Eurasian countries widen the H1 gap of option 1: the SAE box starts next to 16 of 41 countries and 7
of 17 battlegrounds. Under H2 the boxes start level in countries, with SAE one battleground ahead one step out.

**Phone-first zoom.** The eight-country regions need a 4 x 2 grid: on 844 x 390, with the same assumed 100 px
for the top bar and the hand's handle, each box is about 210 x 145 px, still a large tap target but tighter for
the name and two influence numbers ("United Kingdom", "Azerbaijan"). Sub-Saharan Africa's four boxes and the
Americas' five have room to spare. Zoomed out: 41 badges and two home boxes, the busiest of the three options,
most of them crowded into Europe and the Middle East.

**Closing order (README, C1 to C3).** Regions differ in size and battlegrounds, so the order changes how much of
the board closes:

| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------ | ------------ | ------------ |
| Europe                          | Germany, Poland, Serbia      | 5            | open         | open         |
| Eastern Europe and Central Asia | Ukraine, Georgia, Kazakhstan | 3            | open         | 4            |
| Middle East and North Africa    | Syria, Iraq, Iran            | open         | 5            | 5            |
| Sub-Saharan Africa              | Nigeria, South Africa        | open         | 3            | open         |
| East and South Asia             | Japan, Taiwan, India         | 4            | open         | 3            |
| The Americas                    | Mexico, Venezuela, Brazil    | open         | 4            | open         |

| Order | Battlegrounds open at 3 / 4 / 5 | Countries closed at Brink |
| ----- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| C1    | 14 / 11 / 8                     | 24 of 41                  |
| C2    | 15 / 12 / 9                     | 17 of 41                  |
| C3    | 14 / 11 / 8                     | 24 of 41                  |

In a closed region placing goes on, but influence there can then be removed only by card events, so a lead
held when it closes tends to last.

- **C1** and **C3** close three eight-country regions by Brink. C1's three are the regions holding all 16 of
  the SAE box's H1 neighbours, so at Brink every country next to the SAE box is closed to interventions and realignments; C3 closes
  the Middle East instead of Europe, leaving Poland and Finland open. Both leave Sub-Saharan Africa and the
  Americas open, plus the Middle East (C1) or Europe (C3).
- **C2** closes the two smallest regions first, so level 3 and 4 change little on the Eurasian landmass, and at
  Brink 24 countries and 9 battlegrounds are still open to interventions, so Escalation is easiest to push on to
  a crisis under C2.

**Scenario-data work for the rules lane.** 41 country entries (42 with a Crimea box), 61 adjacency pairs
between countries, two home-box neighbour lists (3 and 16 under H1), three region IDs for the closing order, and
the starting numbers for Taiwan and Crimea. The most artwork of the three: eight long links, two of them across
the South China Sea and the Indian Ocean. The content check W7 map option A
describes (symmetric adjacency, every country reachable from a home box) passes as it stands.
